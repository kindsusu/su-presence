# Decision Record — Splitting Daum/Kakao into the KEO Lane

[한국어](2026-09-11-keo-lane.ko.md)

**Decision date** 2026-09-11 · **Status** Applied · **Impact** `crawl.py` `generate.py` `report.py` `collect.py` · `lanes/` · `ops/coverage.md` · audit scorecard

After running the full pipeline twice on a single rental-car site, **we split Daum/Kakao off from
the NEO (Naver) lane into its own independent lane.** This document is the reasoning behind that
call.

> Why keep this record: code preserves "what was done," but not **"why it was done that way."**
> This is the document to read when a proposal to merge the lanes back together comes up again.

---

## One-line summary

**Naver and Daum differ in crawler tokens, ownership-verification method, registration flow, and
the model that writes the AI summary.** While the two were bundled into one cell, **both sides
were leaking in audits.**

---

## Why they were bundled together

At first we treated this as one lump called "Korean search." `lanes/naver.md` §7 placed
Daum/Kakao as a **subsection**, with a conditional note attached: *"promote to an independent
lane if the workload grows."* That was a reasonable starting point — few tools cover both
domestic search engines, and we weren't confident Daum's market share was large enough to justify
a separate lane.

## What real measurement revealed — a bug, not a threshold

Setting the promotion condition to "when the workload grows" was the wrong call. **The problem
was structural, not volume.** While the two were bundled, all of the following was leaking, and
none of it happened because "the workload grew."

| Where it leaked | What happened |
|---|---|
| `ops/crawlers.md` | The Daum crawler wasn't mentioned **anywhere.** Writing robots.txt from this document drops Daum entirely |
| `generate.py`'s robots draft | Had `Daumoa` only — `DAUM` was missing |
| audit scorecard | `LANES` had no KEO entry, so **Daum problems never showed up in the score** |
| Report's lane description | Lumped together as *"Naver/Daum indexing and AI Briefing,"* so **which side had the problem was invisible** |
| `ops/coverage.md` | The KEO surfaces (web search, AI Summary, Webmaster Tools, Kakao Map NAP) weren't in the list |

## Four things are actually different

### 1. Crawler tokens — and Daum has two

Naver has one: `Yeti`. Daum is split between the official documentation's `DAUM` and the
industry-practice `Daumoa`.

**robots.txt applies only the single most specific UA group.** List `Daumoa` alone, and requests
arriving as `DAUM` never meet that group — they fall through to the `*` group. Bundle the two
into one lane called "domestic crawlers" and this difference disappears from view.

### 2. Ownership verification — the finding design diverges

| | Naver | Daum |
|---|---|---|
| Method | `naver-site-verification` **meta tag** | **Site URL + PIN**, a robots.txt comment |
| Crawl-detectable | Yes (no meta tag → suspect unconnected) | **No** — can prove neither "registered" nor "unregistered" |
| Tool handling | `NAVER_VERIFY_MISSING` (warn) | **Not turned into a finding** → human-checked item |

This was the deciding factor. **Turning an absence into a finding means writing down *not
observed* as *something is wrong*.** Put both in the same lane and the same rule ends up applied
to both, but the two engines differ in verifiability from the start. This surfaced in practice
when we added a Daum ownership-verification finding and it **broke the clean-site test.**

### 3. Registration flow — one of them expires

- Naver: Search Advisor, **account**-based, never expires
- Daum: search registration (top page of the primary domain only) + Webmaster Tools (**PIN**,
  **resets after 24 months** once you accept the terms)

**Put an expiring procedure and a non-expiring one on the same checklist, and the renewal
reminder disappears.**

### 4. A different party writes the AI summary

Daum was **acquired by Upstage in May 2026**, and its AI Summary runs on Upstage's **Solar**, not
a Kakao model. Only Kakao Map and Kakao Talk Channel remain with Kakao, so **the local data is
joined by a contract between two companies** — if that link breaks, Kakao Map NAP handling
becomes void entirely.

Put this in the same lane as Naver's AI Briefing, and there is no place to check this risk on a
quarterly basis.

### Aside — the favored channel is different too

What Daum favors is **Tistory** (its own search-registration guide says so directly). The logic
is the same inside/outside two-track split as Naver's, but **the target platform differs.**
In the September 2026 measurement, Daum's Unified Web top results actually skewed toward
`blog.naver.com`, and neither Brunch nor Daum Cafe were observed — in other words, **"it's the
Kakao ecosystem, so Brunch/Cafe" turned out not to be true.**

---

## Alternatives considered and why they were rejected

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Keep it as a subsection inside NEO | **The scorecard is per-lane**, so scores don't separate. Even with Daum blocked, it stays buried inside the NEO score |
| Bundle Naver and Daum under one `KR` | This just reproduces the current problem — the two engines' verification rules differ, but get merged into one score |
| Drop Daum entirely | Real measurement confirmed **3/3 exposure on brand-term search**. Not measuring a channel that reaches users just creates a blind spot |
| Defer promotion "until the workload grows" | The problem was **structural**, not volume. It kept leaking the whole time we deferred |

## Cost paid

- Adding a lane key to the audit scorecard required **compatibility with the old `audit.json`**
  → made scorecard lookups permissive (`.get(lane) or {...}`)
- A test that hardcoded the lane count as a constant broke → replaced with `len(report.LANES)`
- Korean and English documentation each gained one file. **The English mirror was missed at
  first**, so we added a test that checks every lane has both a Korean and an English document
  (`tests/test_docs_match_tools.py`)

## How this prevents recurrence

`tests/test_docs_match_tools.py` guards three things.

1. The full robots UA list in `ops/crawlers.md` **==** `generate.py`'s `UA_GROUPS`
2. Every UA that `crawl.py` checks policy for **must be documented somewhere**
3. Every lane in `crawl.LANES` **must have both a Korean and an English document**

It also checks that both Daum tokens (`DAUM` and `Daumoa`) appear in all three of `crawl.py`,
`generate.py`, and the documentation. We **confirmed it actually fails** by deleting the Daum
block from the documentation.
